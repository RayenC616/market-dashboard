Executive Summary

U.S. equities fell broadly on Tuesday, September 1: S&P 500 -0.71% to 7,631.47, Nasdaq -1.03% to 26,099.77, Dow -0.79% to 52,767. WTI crude spiked +5.20% to $90.22/bbl and Brent +4.6% to $94.65/bbl after fresh U.S. strikes on Iranian targets near the Strait of Hormuz, reviving the "no war, no peace" risk premium in oil. The 10-year Treasury yield pushed to a year-to-date high near 4.80% as global bond yields (Japan's 10-year hit 3% for the first time since 1996) rose in tandem, pressuring rate-sensitive cyclicals. Cross-asset tone was classic risk-off-with-a-commodity-bid: energy, utilities and healthcare outperformed while tech, consumer cyclicals and industrials lagged; gold fell -1.90% to $4,396.40/oz and silver -2.42% to $65.37/oz as the stronger dollar and higher yields dulled havens. Macro data (JOLTS job openings 7.271M vs. 7.30M est.; ISM Manufacturing PMI 54.6 vs. 55.2 est.) came in slightly soft but kept the economy in expansion.

Two U.S. data releases landed on September 1. The ISM Manufacturing PMI for August registered 54.6%, down 1.0 point from July's 55.6% and below the 55.2% consensus estimate (Institute for Supply Management), marking the eighth straight month of expansion (a reading above 47.5% signals overall-economy growth) but with New Orders decelerating to 53.7 from 56.7 and the Prices Paid index holding at an elevated 71.1 — evidence that input-cost inflation, partly linked to the oil-price spike and the ongoing Iran conflict, remains in the pipeline. Separately, July JOLTS job openings rose to 7.271 million from a downwardly revised 7.182 million in June, but came in below the 7.30 million consensus (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics via Trading Economics); hires and total separations were little changed at roughly 5.1 million, with quits (3.1 million) little changed — consistent with a labor market that is cooling gradually rather than cracking. Also released: the S&P Global U.S. Manufacturing PMI (final) for August came in at 53.4, below the 53.9 estimate and up modestly from July's 53.2, and July construction spending fell -0.5% m/m versus a flat consensus. The week's key catalyst — August nonfarm payrolls and the unemployment rate — is due Friday, September 4, with ADP's private payrolls estimate and the Fed's Beige Book scheduled for Wednesday, September 2.

Cross-Cutting Issues to Watch

1. Strait of Hormuz escalation and oil supply risk. The U.S. and Iran traded strikes for the first time in over a month: U.S. Central Command confirmed strikes on IRGC targets in Iran on September 1 following a September 1 (UK Maritime Trade Operations report) attack on a tanker transiting the Strait of Hormuz and an earlier Iranian strike on two U.S. bases in Jordan. Strait transits remain heavily constrained (107 crossings Aug 24-30 vs. 121 the prior week, per MarineTraffic), and analysts at MST Financial describe a "no war, no peace" dynamic that could persist "well into 2027." Investors should monitor for further tit-for-tat strikes, which have driven Brent above $94/bbl and WTI above $90/bbl intraday, with direct pass-through to energy-sector equities, airline/transport costs, and headline inflation prints. (Al Jazeera, CNBC, September 1, 2026)
2. Fed policy repricing after Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks. Following Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish August 28 Jackson Hole speech, CME FedWatch-implied odds of a September rate hike have risen sharply — from roughly 36-40% pre-speech to approximately 56-66% as of September 1 — while December hike odds are near 89%. This repricing has pushed the 10-year Treasury yield to a year-to-date high near 4.80% and contributed to gold's pullback (-1.90% on Sept 1 to $4,396.40/oz) and broad pressure on rate-sensitive equity sectors (consumer cyclicals, technology, industrials). Watch for confirmation or pushback in this week's data — ADP payrolls (Sept 2), ISM Services PMI (Sept 3), and nonfarm payrolls (Sept 4) — ahead of the FOMC's September meeting. (CNBC, Reuters, Washington Post, August 28-31, 2026)
3. Global bond-yield surge outside the U.S. Japan's 10-year government bond yield reached 3% on September 1 for the first time since 1996, while yields in Germany, the UK and France are also near multi-year highs, per Edward Jones. The Bank of Japan meets later this month with analysts expecting a hike. A synchronized global rise in sovereign yields — if it continues — raises cross-asset volatility risk (equity duration sensitivity, currency carry unwinds) and warrants monitoring of the BOJ decision and any follow-through in JGB yields. (Edward Jones, Charles Schwab, September 1, 2026)
4. California utility regulatory risk (PG&E, Edison International). On Monday, August 31, California utilities PG&E (-20%) and Edison International (-23%) sold off sharply after the California legislature advanced wildfire-liability legislation that offered less protection to electricity providers than hoped; Bank of America downgraded both names in response. This is a sector-specific regulatory overhang for California-exposed utilities that could resurface as the legislation progresses, even as the broader Utilities sector (XLU +0.78% on Sept 1) traded as a defensive outperformer. (Investrade Closing Recap, September 1, 2026)
