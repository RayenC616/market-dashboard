Session Review — Friday, August 28, 2026

Executive Summary

U.S. equities closed slightly lower Friday as Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote hardened rate-hike expectations: the S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,711.76, the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.52% to 26,402.42 and the Dow was roughly flat (-0.02%) at 53,559.99, even as all three indexes still posted weekly gains (S&P +0.5%, Nasdaq +0.8%). Warsh said underlying inflation (PCE +3.7% y/y, core +3.3%) remains "too high" and that the Fed has "work to do," pushing September rate-hike odds to roughly 57.5% from about 35% the day prior. Cross-asset reaction was classically hawkish: gold sank 1.7% to $4,529.90/oz and silver fell 2.4%, the 2-year Treasury yield jumped over 6bp, while agricultural futures (wheat +5.6%, corn +5.1%) extended a Black Sea-driven rally to fresh multi-year highs. Asia-Pacific markets closed mixed ahead of the speech — Nikkei +0.4%, Hang Seng +0.1% — while South Korea's KOSPI dropped 1.8% on profit-taking after a blistering run; European equities firmed, with the STOXX 600 up 0.5%.

The dominant macro event of the session was Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh's keynote address at the Kansas City Fed's Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium — his first as chairman and delivered on the 100th day of his tenure. Warsh did not commit to explicit forward guidance or a reaction function, but stated plainly that "the Fed's predominant focus right now should be on prices" and that the central bank has "work to do" before being confident underlying inflation is returning to target. He cited the PCE price index (the Fed's preferred gauge) at +3.7% over the trailing 12 months and +4.1% annualized over the trailing six months, both well above the 2% objective, while noting labor markets remain "quite stable" with unemployment at 4.1%.

This speech followed the July Personal Income and Outlays report released Aug. 26, 2026 by the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis: headline PCE inflation printed at Actual 3.7% y/y (Estimate 3.6%, Prior 3.7% in June), topping consensus by 0.1pp, while core PCE (ex-food & energy) printed at Actual 3.3% y/y (Estimate 3.3%, Prior 3.3%), in line with forecasts; on a monthly basis, both headline and core PCE rose 0.2%.

The most recent labor-market data remains the July employment report (released Aug. 7, 2026): nonfarm payrolls fell by 23,000 (Actual -23K vs. Estimate +83K, Prior revised), an unexpected loss, while the unemployment rate eased to Actual 4.1% (Estimate 4.2%, Prior 4.2%). Markets initially interpreted the soft payrolls print as rate-hike-negative, but Warsh's hawkish framing on inflation on Aug. 28 reversed that read, pushing CME FedWatch odds of a 25bp September hike to roughly 57.5% from about 35.4% the day before the speech (versus 55.8% a month earlier). The next major scheduled release is the August employment situation report, due Friday, Sept. 4, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. ET.

September rate-decision odds have swung sharply hawkish. CME FedWatch-implied odds of a 25bp hike at the Sept. FOMC meeting jumped to ~57.5% immediately after Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks, up from ~35.4% the prior day and above the ~55.8% seen a month ago; Kalshi prediction-market odds of a hike rose to 48% from near-zero expectations of a hold pre-speech. The Aug. 7 jobs report (nonfarm payrolls -23K) had pointed the other way, so the Sept. 4 August payrolls report and any further Fed commentary will be pivotal in confirming or reversing this repricing. The Strait of Hormuz/US-Iran conflict has now run six months and continues to distort oil-supply flows, even as headline crude prices stay contained (WTI ~$83, down modestly on the day). Reporting indicates Iranian oil exports have been cut roughly 85% from pre-war levels to around 250,000 barrels/day, keeping the futures curve in steep backwardation. A resolution or escalation on this front is a binary catalyst for energy and broader risk sentiment. The collapse of the Advent/Stripe-led ~$50 billion buyout pursuit of PayPal (reported Aug. 27-28) sent PayPal shares down roughly 16% and removes what would have been one of the largest-ever leveraged buyouts from the M&A pipeline. Watch for read-through to fintech and payments-sector M&A appetite more broadly. (Source: Bloomberg, bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/advent-stripe-consortium-is-said-to-drop-pursuit-of-paypal)
AI-trade sustainability is back in focus after a volatile week: Nvidia's ~9% post-earnings jump lifted the S&P/Nasdaq Thursday, but the broader semiconductor complex (VanEck Semiconductor ETF, SMH) still fell over 3% on the week as Marvell's underwhelming guidance and rate-hike jitters weighed on high-multiple names. With the AI-capex buildout cited explicitly by Warsh as a driver of resilient business investment (4-quarter equipment/intangibles growth near 9%, the fastest since 2021), the durability of that trade into a potentially higher-rate regime is a key swing factor for tech-sector leadership.
