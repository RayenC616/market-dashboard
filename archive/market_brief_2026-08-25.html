Session Review — Tuesday, August 25, 2026

Executive Summary

US equities closed higher Tuesday, Aug 25: the S&P 500 rose 0.32% to 7,677.28, the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.66% to 26,151.30, and the Dow added 0.30% to 53,577.40, extending the Dow's win streak to three sessions. Semiconductor names rebounded (SOX +1.4%) ahead of Nvidia's Wednesday earnings, the marquee catalyst for the AI trade. Crude oil sold off sharply (WTI -3.1% to $82.36; Brent -3.9% to $88.58) as US sanctions on Iran stopped short of directly targeting China, easing supply-disruption fears, while the 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.638%. Cross-asset tone was risk-on for equities and bonds but risk-off for energy; gold was little changed near three-month highs. Asia closed mixed-to-higher (Nikkei +0.50%, Kospi +0.68%, Hang Seng -0.02%) and Europe's STOXX 600 added 0.35% to a third straight gain.

Cross-Cutting Issues to Watch

1. US-Canada trade tensions escalating. Ottawa unveiled retaliatory tariffs of 15%-50% on roughly US$20 billion (C$27.6bn) of American goods — steel, dairy, appliances, farm equipment, pulp/paper and electronics — effective Sept. 8, after the two countries' trade talks collapsed (Washington Post, CNBC, Al Jazeera). President Trump responded by threatening to raise the existing 25% US auto tariff on Canadian vehicles to 50%. Markets have so far shrugged this off, but escalation risk into the September deadline bears watching, particularly for autos, steel and building-materials exposure.
2. US Treasury's 'economic D-Day' sanctions campaign against Iran. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent unveiled sweeping new sanctions Monday (Aug 24) targeting Iran's oil, shipping, digital-asset, aviation and gold sectors, but notably stopped short of directly penalizing China — the largest buyer of Iranian crude (Washington Post, Axios, NYT). Oil sold off Tuesday as the immediate escalation risk eased, but the administration has signaled further steps are possible; any move that does target Chinese buyers would be a fresh catalyst for crude and could reignite US-China friction.
3. Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole address as Fed Chair (Friday, Aug 28/29). Investors are watching for signals on the Fed's approach to interest rates and inflation measurement — Warsh has previously criticized the PCE framework the Fed uses as its inflation benchmark (Investopedia, Reuters, Guardian). Money markets are currently pricing roughly one 25bp move by year-end; any deviation from Warsh's 'less-is-more' communication style could move rates and, by extension, rate-sensitive equity sectors (Utilities, Real Estate) meaningfully.
4. Nvidia earnings (Wednesday, Aug 26, after the close) as the next test for the AI trade. After a seven-session Nvidia losing streak and a broader semiconductor selloff earlier in the week, Tuesday's rebound (SOX +1.4%, Nvidia +2.2%) suggests markets are positioning for a strong print, but any signs of decelerating growth or capex commentary from hyperscalers could reignite concerns about stretched AI valuations (Reuters, Investopedia).
