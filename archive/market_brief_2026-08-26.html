Session Review — 2026-08-26

Executive Summary

U.S. equities finished essentially flat on Wednesday, August 26, with the S&P 500 down 0.02% to 7,675.70, the Dow off 0.21% to 53,463.88, and the Nasdaq Composite down 0.08% to 26,130.20, as markets digested a hotter-than-expected annual PCE inflation print (3.7% YoY headline vs. 3.6% est.) ahead of Nvidia's after-the-close earnings. The second GDP estimate confirmed 1.5% Q2 annualized growth with an upward revision to consumer spending (3.4%). Cross-asset tone was mixed-to-risk-off: gold fell 0.86% to $4,598 and silver, copper and aluminium all declined as the firmer inflation data lifted the 10-year Treasury yield to roughly 4.66-4.70%, while WTI crude eased 0.16% to $82.23 on easing Iran-Oman Strait of Hormuz tensions. Asia and Europe closed broadly higher, led by the Nikkei 225 (+0.62%) and KOSPI (+0.97%).

The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, the PCE Price Index, rose 0.2% m/m in July, putting annual headline inflation at 3.7% (vs. 3.6% consensus, 3.7% prior) per the Commerce Department/BEA release covered by CNBC. Core PCE (ex-food and energy) was in line at 0.2% m/m and 3.3% y/y, matching both consensus and the prior reading. Personal income rose a stronger-than-expected 0.4% (vs. 0.2% est.) while personal spending rose 0.2% (in line). Separately, the BEA's second estimate of Q2 2026 GDP confirmed 1.5% annualized growth (unchanged from the advance estimate, down from 2.1% in Q1), but with an upward revision to real consumer spending to 3.4% (from an initial 3.2%) and to nonresidential fixed investment (8.5% annualized), per Quartz's coverage of the release. July durable goods orders rose 1.1% m/m, beating the 0.5% consensus and extending a string of upside surprises in the series, per Trading Economics' U.S. economic calendar. Markets are pricing roughly a 1-in-3 probability of a Fed rate move at the September 15-16 FOMC meeting; the Fed does not meet formally in August but Chair Kevin Warsh is scheduled to deliver his first Jackson Hole keynote address on Friday, August 28, which investors expect to offer further policy signal ahead of September.

Cross-Cutting Issues to Watch

1. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote (Friday, Aug 28) - Warsh's first major policy speech as Fed Chair comes just after a hotter-than-expected annual PCE print and with the 10-year Treasury yield near cycle highs (~4.66-4.70%). Markets are pricing roughly a 36-38% probability of a September rate move; any signal on the Fed's tolerance for above-target inflation versus a slowing labor market could move rates, the dollar and gold sharply into the September 15-16 FOMC meeting. (Sources: CNBC, Equiti Markets)

2. Nvidia earnings as an AI-capex referendum - Nvidia reported fiscal Q2 2027 results after Wednesday's close, a release widely framed as a test of the broader AI investment cycle after the stock's seven-day, ~7.5% pre-earnings skid - its longest losing streak since 2022. Guidance on hyperscaler capex and margins is likely to set the tone for chip and software names (and correlated names like AMD) into the September trading session. (Source: Charles Schwab market update)

3. Healthcare-sector cybersecurity risk - Boston Scientific's disclosed cyberattack, which disrupted global order processing and shipping and coincided with an FDA-related product recall, is the latest in a string of cyberattacks targeting the healthcare sector this year. Investors should watch for read-through to peer medtech names and for confirmation of whether the incident has a material financial impact once the company provides further disclosure. (Sources: CNBC, The Motley Fool)

4. Strait of Hormuz / Iran-Oman negotiations - Crude prices have been volatile for weeks on the Iran conflict; reports that Iran and Oman are nearing an agreement to reopen safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz drove WTI to a two-week low this week. A confirmed deal would remove a meaningful geopolitical risk premium from oil and could pressure energy-sector equities even as it eases broader inflation risk; a breakdown in talks would have the opposite effect. (Source: Reuters, via search coverage)
