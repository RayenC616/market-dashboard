Executive Summary

U.S. equities closed lower Monday as the S&P 500 fell 0.33% to 7,686.14, the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.12% to 26,370.89, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 374.09 points (-0.70%) to 53,185.90, after the U.S. and Iran exchanged military strikes for the first time in a month. WTI crude jumped 2.83% to $85.76 and Brent rose 2.71% to $90.49 on the Hormuz-area escalation, while the 10-year Treasury yield climbed 3.6bp to 4.758% (highest since Jan 15, 2025) as Fed rate-hike odds for September rose to 57-65% following Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole remarks. Cross-asset signal: risk-off in equities and duration, oil bid on geopolitics, gold soft (spot -0.1% to ~$4,431-4,448) even as safe-haven demand stayed muted. Despite Monday's losses, all three major U.S. averages closed August with gains (S&P +2.6%, Nasdaq +3.9%, Dow's fifth straight monthly advance).

Cross-Cutting Issues to Watch

1. U.S.-Iran military escalation and Strait of Hormuz risk. The U.S. struck two Iranian missile launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz on August 30 — the first acknowledged U.S. strike on Iranian positions since late July — and Iran retaliated with strikes on U.S. bases in Jordan; President Trump further threatened Iran's Kharg Island oil export terminal. With the conflict now in its sixth month and Hormuz vessel traffic already disrupted, further supply-side shocks to crude (WTI $85.76, Brent $90.49) are the top near-term risk to the inflation outlook and Fed policy path. (Reuters, CNBC)
2. Fed policy pivot toward a September hike. Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech (Aug 28) reset rate-hike odds from roughly 35-40% to 57-65% for the September 16-17 FOMC meeting. Confirmation or reversal hinges on Friday's August payrolls report (consensus +58,000) and the September 11 CPI print — both first-tier catalysts to watch this week. (Reuters, CNBC, USAGOLD)
