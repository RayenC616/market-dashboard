Executive Summary

U.S. equities snapped a two-session slide on Wednesday, September 2, with the S&P 500 rising 0.46% to 7,666.60, the Nasdaq Composite gaining 0.45% to 26,217.83, and the Dow adding 0.56% to 53,061.95; the Russell 2000 outperformed, up 1.13%. The advance came despite the 10-year Treasury yield touching 4.818% intraday — its highest since November 2023 — before easing back to a flat 4.79% close, and despite ADP reporting private payrolls grew just 38,000 in August versus 47,000 expected, the weakest pace since January. Cross-asset signal was risk-on-with-caveats: WTI crude rose a third straight session to $91.01 (+0.88%) and Brent settled above $95/bbl for the first time since late July on renewed US-Iran strikes, while gold slipped toward its 50%-retracement technical level near $4,370. Overseas, the same bond-yield shock hit harder: Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 2.85% to 64,325.64 as the 10-year JGB yield hit 3% for the first time since 1996, and South Korea's KOSPI dropped 4.0% to 6,562.72.

The session's dominant macro release was the ADP National Employment Report for August: private payrolls rose just 38,000, below the 47,000 consensus estimate and the weakest print since January (prior: 46,000 in July), per ADP Research and confirmed by CNBC. The composition was notably narrow — education and health services added roughly 45,000 positions while manufacturing shed about 17,000 and professional/business services lost roughly 16,000 — a mixed lead-in to Friday's Bureau of Labor Statistics nonfarm-payrolls report. Factory orders for July came in at +0.9%, above the +0.6% estimate and a sharp reversal from June's -0.2%, driven by a rebound in transportation orders. The Federal Reserve's Beige Book, published at 2 p.m. ET, described "modest" economic growth and "moderate" price increases nationally, with business contacts citing heightened uncertainty tied to energy costs, policy, and the ongoing US-Iran conflict; employment effects from AI adoption were mixed, with strong wage growth in manufacturing and construction trades. EIA data showed crude inventories fell 4.5 million barrels, with refineries running at 98% of capacity. Markets moved the implied odds of a September 25-basis-point Fed rate hike to roughly 66%, up from about 40% a week earlier, according to Trading Economics, even as New York Fed President John Williams told CNBC there were "no clear signs right now" that a September hike is needed, suggesting the yield surge may reflect economic strength rather than inflation expectations. The Fed's FOMC next meets September 15-16.

Cross-Cutting Issues to Watch

1. Renewed US-Iran conflict and its energy/inflation spillover. Overnight US-Iran strikes pushed Brent crude above $95/bbl for the first time since late July and kept WTI near $91, while U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright said a record 17 million-plus barrels transited the Strait of Hormuz on Monday — a claim ship-tracker Kpler disputes, reporting a sharp decline in vessel traffic the same day. The divergence between official and independent shipping data is worth monitoring for early signs of a genuine supply disruption. (Source: Yahoo Finance)
2. Global government-bond yield surge. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield hit 4.818% intraday, its highest since November 2023, while Japan's 10-year JGB yield crossed 3% for the first time since 1996 and German 10-year Bund yields hit a 15-year high near 3.38%. Rising sovereign borrowing costs are pressuring rate-sensitive equities (real estate, growth tech) globally and raising financing-cost concerns for governments and consumers alike heading into the September 15-16 FOMC meeting. (Source: WSJ)
3. AI-capex earnings season delivering a mixed verdict. Broadcom beat on revenue (AI semiconductor revenue +221% y/y) and earnings but fell 3.5% after-hours on fourth-quarter guidance ($34.8B) landing under the $35.03B Street estimate, echoing a similar 12.6%-single-day drop in June when it declined to raise its FY2027 AI-chip target. By contrast, Snowflake jumped 21% after-hours on a raised FY2027 revenue outlook. Watch for confirmation or contradiction of the AI-infrastructure demand narrative from remaining reports this earnings cycle. (Source: Ringside Post-Bell)
4. California utility wildfire-liability overhang. PG&E and Edison International both sold off (-5.2% and -6.1% respectively) after Senate Bill 492 — a wildfire-liability rewrite — died without a vote when California's legislative session ended, and PG&E separately deferred $2 billion of planned 2027 capital spending. JPMorgan cut its Edison International price target to $61 from $82, flagging renewed liability risk; this is a sector-specific regulatory risk worth tracking into any special legislative session or 2027 rate-case filings. (Source: Ringside Post-Bell)
