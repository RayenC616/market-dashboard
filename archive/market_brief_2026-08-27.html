Executive Summary

U.S. equities closed higher Thursday, August 27, led decisively by technology: the S&P 500 gained 0.72% to 7,730.99 and the Nasdaq Composite rose 1.57% to 26,541.35, while the Dow added a more modest 0.2%. The rally was powered by a post-earnings surge in Nvidia (+8%) after the AI chipmaker beat estimates and guided above consensus, reinforced by strong prints from Salesforce and CrowdStrike and news that Nvidia agreed to buy Hugging Face for $12.9 billion. Ten of eleven S&P sectors fell even as the index rose, underscoring narrow, mega-cap-driven leadership. Cross-asset signal was mixed-to-risk-on: WTI crude rose to $83.53 (+1.6%) on Iran-Oman Strait of Hormuz tensions, gold was little changed (+0.25%) near record territory, and the 10-year Treasury yield ticked up to 4.67% as markets positioned ahead of Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Friday Jackson Hole keynote.

The unemployment rate stands at 4.1%, just below the Fed's estimated longer-run full-employment rate of 4.2%.

The more consequential macro print of the week — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, core PCE for July — was released the prior day (August 26): core PCE held at 3.3% year-on-year, matching consensus, while headline PCE ran hotter at 3.7% y/y (source: Vantage Markets; NYTimes). Both remain well above the Fed's 2% target, a backdrop that looms over the week's main event: Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote address, scheduled for Friday, August 28 at 10:00 a.m. ET, where markets will look for signals on the future rate path amid Warsh's stated reluctance to offer forward guidance.

Separately, South Korea's central bank delivered a second consecutive 25-basis-point rate hike, lifting its base rate to 3.00% — its highest level since January 2025 — as it continues to combat elevated core inflation (source: Reuters, CNBC). The move helps explain the KOSPI's outsized 64.0% YTD and 114.4% one-year gains in the price table below.

Cross-Cutting Issues to Watch

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote (Friday, August 28, 10:00 a.m. ET) — Warsh has removed forward guidance from post-meeting statements and reviewed Fed communications practices; with core PCE still at 3.3% y/y against a 2% target, his framing of the inflation-versus-growth tradeoff will set the tone for rate expectations into year-end. (Sources: Fox Business, Reuters)
Strait of Hormuz transit dispute between Iran and Oman — the two sides are edging toward a deal on a shipping corridor and transit fee/toll structure covering roughly a fifth of global oil and gas flows; the US was not party to the negotiation and is unlikely to accept terms that don't restore free passage. Continued vessel strikes (including two Abu Dhabi National Oil Co. tankers hit this week) keep a geopolitical risk premium embedded in crude, evident in WTI's push toward $84. (Source: Fortune)
Nvidia's $12.9 billion agreement to acquire Hugging Face — expands Nvidia's reach into open-source AI model distribution (including llama.cpp) just as the company reported blockbuster Q2 results and guided demand for AI infrastructure to remain strong into next year; regulatory and integration risk warrants monitoring as the AI-capex investment cycle broadens beyond hyperscalers. (Source: CNBC)
Bank of Korea's back-to-back rate hike to 3.00% — a hawkish outlier among major central banks tightening against still-elevated core inflation, in contrast to the Fed's holding pattern; the policy divergence is a factor behind KOSPI's YTD outperformance (+64.0%) and bears watching for spillover into regional FX and capital flows. (Sources: Reuters, CNBC)
