Cross-Cutting Issues to Watch

Middle East escalation and the $100 oil level. Brent crude's break above $100/bbl for the first time since May 26 — triggered by Houthi attacks on two Saudi Arabian tankers in the Red Sea — has reopened the risk of a broader regional conflict. President Trump told Axios he is "considering a massive attack" on Iran, and U.S. Central Command has completed 13 consecutive nights of strikes on Iranian targets. RBC Capital Markets flagged the possibility of Brent testing the 2022 Russia/Ukraine-war high of $128/bbl, or even the 2008 peak of $146/bbl, in a worst-case regional-war scenario. Investors should monitor shipping-route disruption risk (Strait of Hormuz, Red Sea) and downstream effects on gasoline prices and consumer spending. (Source: NBC News, Al Jazeera, The National News, CNBC)

AI-capex fatigue among hyperscalers. Alphabet's and Tesla's guidance hikes — Alphabet to $195-205B and Tesla to over $25B for 2026 — triggered the sharpest one-day pullback across Magnificent Seven names (Amazon -4.5%, Meta -3.5%) since April 2025, per Yahoo Finance. With hyperscaler AI capex tracking toward $700B+ industry-wide this year, watch upcoming earnings (Microsoft, Meta, Amazon) for signs the market's patience with negative free cash flow is wearing thin, and any incremental widening of the "haves vs. have-nots" divide between chip/power infrastructure plays (which rallied) and the capex-funding hyperscalers (which sold off). (Source: Investopedia, Yahoo Finance)

Rising Treasury yields and Fed rate-hike repricing. The 10-year yield's move to 4.71% — its highest since January 2025 — reflects a market now pricing a 36% chance of a Fed rate hike at next week's meeting (up from 12% a week ago) and an 82% chance of a hike by September (up from 52%), according to CME FedWatch data cited by Investopedia. A sustained oil-driven inflation shock colliding with hawkish repricing raises the risk of a policy error; watch next week's FOMC meeting closely for signaling.

New sweeping U.S. tariffs took effect just after midnight ET Friday. The Trump administration imposed 10-12.5% tariffs on 60 trading partners over alleged forced-labor violations, covering more than 99% of U.S. trade, replacing the expiring temporary 10% global tariff. The revenue and trade-flow impact is not yet quantified by USTR; watch for retaliatory measures and corporate guidance updates citing the new levies in the weeks ahead. (Source: CNBC)
